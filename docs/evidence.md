# Evidence-to-design map

Sources were selected in this order: official robot assets, peer-reviewed robotics papers, the latest available primary preprint version, and official Isaac Lab/ROS documentation. The papers motivate the design; they do not establish that the chosen X2 parameters are optimal.

## Primary sources

1. Huang et al., **Learning Humanoid Standing-up Control across Diverse Postures**, Robotics: Science and Systems 2025, DOI 10.15607/RSS.2025.XXI.064. [Proceedings and paper](https://www.roboticsproceedings.org/rss21/p064.html).
2. He et al., **Learning Getting-Up Policies for Real-World Humanoid Robots**, Robotics: Science and Systems 2025, DOI 10.15607/RSS.2025.XXI.063. [Proceedings and paper](https://www.roboticsproceedings.org/rss21/p063.html).
3. Gaspard et al., **FRASA: An End-to-End Reinforcement Learning Agent for Fall Recovery and Stand Up of Humanoid Robots**, arXiv:2410.08655, version 3 revised 4 November 2025. [Abstract and revision history](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.08655).
4. Xu et al., **Unified Humanoid Fall-Safety Policy from a Few Demonstrations**, arXiv:2511.07407, 2025. [Preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.07407). This recent preprint is used only to scope hardware-safety follow-up, not as validation of this policy.
5. Schulman et al., **Proximal Policy Optimization Algorithms**, arXiv:1707.06347, 2017. [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/1707.06347).
6. Hwangbo et al. (ETH Zurich Robotic Systems Lab), **Learning Agile and Dynamic Motor Skills for Legged Robots**, *Science Robotics* 4(26), 2019, DOI 10.1126/scirobotics.aau5872. [ETH research record and accepted manuscript](https://www.research-collection.ethz.ch/items/63b8fb50-299f-4995-bd1d-63a9f2b7ba75). It is quadrupedal rather than humanoid evidence, so it supports only the contact-rich self-righting, position-target and sim-to-real engineering choices.
7. Eßer et al. (Fraunhofer IML and MIT), **Action Space Design in Reinforcement Learning for Robot Motor Skills**, *Conference on Robot Learning 2024*, PMLR 270, 2025. [Peer-reviewed proceedings](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v270/esser25a.html). The study shows that action representation materially changes learning and that joint-position targets are a common, defensible choice for legged systems; it does not claim one action space is universally best.
8. Peng, Bao and Zhou (University College London), **Gait-Conditioned Reinforcement Learning with Multi-Phase Curriculum for Humanoid Locomotion**, *IEEE-RAS Humanoids 2025*, DOI 10.1109/HUMANOIDS65713.2025.11203058. [UCL manuscript](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/10219246/1/2025_Humanoid_Conference_Paper-13.pdf). It supports routing stage-specific objectives and introducing difficulty progressively; its locomotion results are not treated as direct fall-recovery evidence.
9. AgiBotTech, **official AgiBot X2 URDF models**. [Repository](https://github.com/AgibotTech/agibot_x2_urdf). Reviewed `main` commit `60c5de582c523cd188f563819e62d34cfdc3d2d0` dated 8 September 2026 is pinned by the fetch/import scripts. Its X2 Ultra v1.3.0 update corrects mesh filename case and revises documented joint ranges; those current upstream values are used unchanged.
10. AgiBot, **X2 coordinate-system and joint-limit documentation**. [Coordinate system](https://x2-aimdk.agibot.com/en/latest/about_agibot_X2/coordinate_system.html) and [joint limits](https://x2-aimdk.agibot.com/en/latest/about_agibot_X2/joint_name_and_limit.html).
11. NVIDIA, **Isaac Sim URDF importer**. [Official importer documentation](https://docs.isaacsim.omniverse.nvidia.com/latest/importer_exporter/ext_isaacsim_asset_importer_urdf.html).
12. NVIDIA, **Isaac Lab reinforcement learning and manager-based task documentation**. [RL concepts](https://isaac-sim.github.io/IsaacLab/develop/source/concepts/reinforcement_learning.html) and [manager-based environment guide](https://isaac-sim.github.io/IsaacLab/main/source/how-to/create_manager_base_env.html).
13. Open Robotics, **ROS 2 Humble interfaces and QoS**. [Topics, services and actions](https://docs.ros.org/en/humble/Concepts/Basic/About-Interfaces.html) and [QoS settings](https://docs.ros.org/en/humble/Concepts/Intermediate/About-Quality-of-Service-Settings.html).

## Traceability

| Design decision | Evidence | Repository implementation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Use the official X2 Ultra v1.3.0 model and simplified collision URDF | AgiBot identifies v1.3.0 as the original flagship X2 Ultra and v1.4.0 as X2 Ultra -N; the assignment does not identify an -N robot | `fetch_agibot_model.sh`, `import_x2_urdf.py`; confirm the physical nameplate before transfer |
| Initialize supine rather than prone | AgiBot documents an FLU body frame: X forward, Y left, Z up; -90° about Y maps the forward/chest axis upward | scalar-last `X2_CFG.init_state.rot=(0, -0.7071, 0, 0.7071)`; live probe checks the transformed forward axis |
| Convert URDF into a repo-local, floating, self-colliding humanoid USD | Isaac Sim 6 documents `URDFImporter`/`URDFImporterConfig`; official importer preserves robot structure and exposes humanoid/floating-base options | `UrdfConverterCfg(fix_base=False, robot_type="Humanoid", self_collision=True)` |
| Prefer simplified collision geometry and inspect the reset after import | He et al. identify accurate collision geometry as central to learning contact-rich getting-up motions, then refine discovered motion for deployability | official simplified-collision URDF; reset inspection remains a required target-workstation check |
| Use dense discovery rewards for pelvis/head height, upward progress, upright posture and foot support | HumanUP Stage-I Appendix Table II publishes these exact reward families and weights for supine getting up | `humanup_base_height()`, `humanup_head_height()`, `humanup_height_increase()`, `humanup_body_upright()`, and `both_feet_when_high()` |
| Activate stable-standing objectives only after reaching the final height stage | HoST divides righting, rising and standing at base-height thresholds and its Table VI gates post-task velocity, orientation and height terms above stage two | four `host_post_*` terms gated at the X2-specific 0.62 m threshold |
| Mix standing starts early and remove them before final training | HumanUP explicitly mixes standing poses into Stage I to accelerate discovery, then trains the deployable task from broader postures | `reset_root_state_recovery_curriculum()` anneals the standing-reset probability from 0.50 to zero by 16,000 control steps; play/evaluation forces it to zero |
| Use an annealed vertical exploration force | HoST begins with a 200 N upward base force and decreases it as the task is learned | `apply_vertical_force_curriculum()` applies world-up force at the pelvis and linearly reaches zero by 24,000 control steps; play/evaluation disables it |
| Reward both feet and penalize every other supporting body | The assignment requires two feet with no other support; FRASA explicitly rewards collision-free target behavior and uses physical stability checks | `both_feet_when_high()`, `unsupported_contacts_when_high()`, `strict_success()` |
| Smooth and bound position targets | HoST identifies smoothness and implicit speed bounds as necessary for deployable motion; ETH's self-righting controller and HumanUP use position targets; the MIT/Fraunhofer study shows the choice must be task-tested | HoST-style `q_target=q_current+0.25a` clipped to the X2 soft limits, plus action-rate and joint-velocity penalties; the strict evaluator decides whether this choice worked on X2 |
| Use 20 Hz policy control with faster physics | FRASA reports a 20 Hz decision process and 100 Hz physical control; faster simulation substeps preserve contact resolution | 200 Hz PhysX with `decimation=10` |
| Require a sustained stable pose | FRASA checks all state errors for 0.5 s before declaring physical stability | ten consecutive 20 Hz strict-success steps |
| Randomize friction, mass/CoM and actuator gains conservatively | HoST and FRASA both randomize contact, inertial and actuator properties for transfer; Isaac Lab exposes these as event terms | startup material, mass, pelvis CoM and gain events |
| Add action, velocity, torque and joint-limit regularization | HoST groups task, style, regularization and post-task rewards; its hardware constraints motivate low-speed, low-effort motion | reward table in `RewardsCfg` |
| Route discovery and completion rewards instead of summing every objective everywhere | HoST and UCL Humanoids 2025 both report that stage/mode-specific objectives reduce interference | height-gated HoST post-task terms; discovery terms remain dense |
| Give every parallel clone a physical floor | Contact-rich learning evidence assumes valid contacts; a shared ground prim must cover every cloned origin | 200 m shared local cuboid covers the configured 2,048-env grid at 2.5 m spacing; a formula test checks the bound |
| Use PPO with clipped updates, GAE and normalized MLP observations | PPO is a standard on-policy clipped objective; Isaac Lab recommends RSL-RL for GPU-parallel robot learning and supplies humanoid PPO examples | `X2RecoveryPPORunnerCfg` |
| Keep service acceptance short and execute asynchronously | ROS documents services as short request/response interactions; the task explicitly mandates `Trigger` rather than an action | service callback sets a pending flag; timer starts the worker after the response |
| Make status durable for late subscribers | ROS 2 transient-local durability retains the last sample for compatible late subscribers | reliable, transient-local `/x2/recovery_status` publisher |
| Separate ROS Humble Python from Isaac Python | This is an engineering constraint observed locally, not a research claim; the runtimes use different Python ABIs | newline JSON over a mode-0600 Unix socket |
| Treat impact mitigation as separate hardware-safety work | Xu et al. jointly study fall prevention, impact reduction and recovery; this assignment begins after the fall and therefore cannot establish safe falling | hardware limitations section; no safe-transfer claim |

## Adaptations and limits

This is an evidence-derived take-home implementation, not a reproduction of any cited system. HoST uses multiple critics and adapts its force from task success; this implementation keeps one PPO critic and a fixed, fully disclosed linear anneal. HumanUP trains Stage I for billions of simulator steps, extracts and slows a discovered trajectory, then performs imitation-based Stage II; the available experiment instead uses its published discovery reward and standing-start mix, followed by several hundred entirely unassisted PPO iterations. FRASA trains a smaller sagittal controller with Cross-Q. The complete 31-joint X2 policy, short training budget and exact assignment success predicate therefore need their own measured result. Domain-randomization ranges must be replaced with identified X2 uncertainty before hardware transfer.
